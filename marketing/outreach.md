# Outreach emails

Short, personal, and about their students or clients, not about you. Send from your own address, one at a time, with the recipient's name and one specific detail about their program. Five a week is plenty. Follow up once after a week; then stop.

## University career centers

**Subject:** Free AI interview prep tool for [University] students

> Hi [Name],
>
> I'm Tim, the developer of NovusOrbit, a free, open-source AI career assistant. I noticed [specific: your career fair next month / your interview prep workshops], and thought it might be a useful extra for your students.
>
> Students upload their CV once, then get interview questions for a specific job with suggested answers drawn from their own experience, resume bullet rewrites, and job matches from live listings. It's free and there's nothing to install: https://www.novusorbit.com/features
>
> If it's helpful, I'd be glad to give a 15-minute demo to your team, or adapt it for something your students struggle with. Either way, I'd value your feedback.
>
> Best,
> Tim

## Career coaches and recruiters (LinkedIn message, under 300 characters)

> Hi [Name], I enjoyed your post on [topic]. I built a free AI tool that preps candidates for a specific job using their own CV. Would you be open to trying it with a client and telling me what's missing? No strings attached.

## When someone says yes

- Ask what their students or clients struggle with most, and build for that.
- Give them a UTM link (see [utm-links.md](utm-links.md)) so you can see the effect.
- After a few weeks, ask for a quote you can use on the site, with permission and their real name.
